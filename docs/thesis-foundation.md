## 1. Historical Context: Why a "Mother Language" Makes Sense

### 1.1 The Tradition of Proto-Language Reconstruction

Since the 19th century, linguists and archaeologists have sought to reconstruct the origins of human languages. Each major language family has its reconstructed proto-language:

- **Proto-Indo-European** (PIE): Ancestral to Sanskrit, Greek, Latin, Germanic, Slavic, Celtic, etc.
- **Proto-Afro-Asiatic**: Ancestral to Semitic (Hebrew, Arabic), Egyptian, Berber, Cushitic
- **Proto-Austronesian**: Ancestral to Malay, Polynesian, Malagasy, Hawaiian
- **Proto-Tupi**: Ancestral to Tupi-Guarani languages of South America
- **Proto-Dravidian**: Ancestral to Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam

Each reconstruction is inherently regional—each family with its own proto-language.

### 1.2 The Deeper Question

The Universal Language Thesis proposes a step beyond regional reconstruction:

> **Not merely isolated families, but a set of universal phonemes and syllables, shared by distant traditions (Harappan, Sanskrit, Hebrew, Tupi-Guarani, Dravidian, Indigenous American languages, Yoruba, etc.), which preserve a common symbolic layer anterior to cultural fragmentation.**

### 1.3 Connections to Prior Research

This thesis converges with several existing scholarly traditions:

1. **The "Adamic Language"**: In Kabbalah, Christian mysticism, and Sufism, there is a tradition of a primordial language given to Adam—a perfect language where words directly expressed the essence of things.

2. **Nostratic and Macro-Family Hypotheses**: Linguists like Greenberg and Ruhlen have proposed macro-families connecting Indo-European, Uralic, Altaic, Dravidian, and more.

3. **Sound Symbolism Research**: Studies show certain sounds associate with similar meanings across unrelated languages (the "bouba-kiki effect").

### 1.4 What Makes This Thesis Different

Unlike traditional reconstruction of basic vocabulary ("father," "mother," "fire," "water"), this thesis proposes a **proto-symbolic vocabulary** comprising:

- **Tokens**: AM, AMA, OM, YA, NU, RA, SHA, TARA, KARAÍ, MBOI, etc.
- **Grammar of Combination**: Suffixes (-NA, -TA, -RA, -MI, -SHI, -YA) generating up to 1000 basic units (the Dictionary 00)

This places the research in the context of deep history, assuming that human language from the beginning mixed **sound, myth, body, cosmology, and mathematics**.
